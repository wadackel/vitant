# Measured and dropped

Each entry says what was tried, what the benchmark measured, and why it went.

Ideas that were implemented, measured on the benchmark, and removed because they did not pay off:

- **Claims of tries sized by what the tries so far took** rather than by the test's unmutated run. In rea a test that takes 4 ms unmutated took 1.4 s a try, mutants leaving it waiting on a child process, and was given out 63 tries to a claim that one worker went through for four minutes. Sized by the tries, rea's `ExecFileOutput.ts` took 1,404 s with `--led --confirm 4` against 1,403 s and 2,491 s without against 2,458 s, round for round the same: its slow tests take seconds unmutated and were given one try a claim already. The six scopes of `bench/tier.ts` kept every verdict and their counts, but for hono `router` starting 183 workers where it had 163.
- **Tries in copies spread over several workers with `--led`,** where a lead made next to other runs of its file counts anyway. One after another they took 214 s of a run of rea's `ExecFileOutput.ts` that took 1,030 s. Given as many workers as their share of the work, the round with them was planned four times over with nothing done, and the file was given up on, every mutant of it pending. Why the other workers made no tries was not looked into; the gain was a sixth of that run at most.
- **Telling from the records whether a try had its file to itself,** rather than from the plan giving the file one worker: each process said when it took a file on, a copy which process it came from, and a try counted as made alone where no other process had its file under way at the time. Read back from the records of earlier runs it never called alone a try the plan had not, and found 77 of 790 leads of a run of rea made alone that the plan had made next to others; but those spared 9 runs made with nothing else running where the next change spared 220, and in a run of rea's `ReverseEngineeringService.ts` with it two leads of 39 counted that would not have, the run taking 656 s against 643 s. It would matter where the plan gives files more workers than they keep busy at once.
- **Workers of their own for a test whose tries take seconds,** with `--led`. A worker goes through a file's tests in order and stops at the first one a mutant leaves broken, and in rea three or four of the workers of `ExecFileOutput.ts` ever got to its test that sleeps 14 s, with thirty tries to make. Given as many workers of its own as its tries came to in half minutes, fourteen tried it at once and its round went from 322 s to 235 s; but the other three tests of the file that sleep were tried a round later instead, and the run took 582 s against 579 s to 628 s.
- **No run of its own for a mutant whose only lead does not count, with `--led`.** A test that failed again without the mutant, or ran out of the time Vitest gives it, is no witness, so under `--led` a run of its file that fails settles nothing: nine such runs of rea's `ExecFileOutput.ts` held eight of twelve workers for 30 s, and five of them were for mutants another test led in the same round. Left to the runs every mutant without a lead gets, that file first and whatever `--confirm` says, the run took 422.1 s against 365.5 s, every mutant's status as before. The round the nine had been in went from 98.1 s to 63.5 s, but their failing was what took those mutants out of further tries: without it a test that takes 6 s tried two of them in a round of its own, 57.3 s, and the pair of unmutated runs that a failure next to other runs asks for came after the last round of whole-file runs rather than next to the tries, that round taking 40.5 s against 7.7 s.
- **Settling a file's failures once that file has nothing else to do,** next to the other files' work, rather than in a round of its own once no file has any. A try counts for its file having one worker, not for an idle machine, and no verdict moved: on rea's `ReverseEngineeringService.ts` every mutant kept its status but one that both fails a test and loops, Killed where it had been Timeout, and `bench/tier.ts`, solid `reactive` and the fixture's 223 mutants agreed with the suite as before. But the round given to settling alone, 25.3 s, was all it saved. The round the first of those tries joined took 55.9 s against 49.6 s, and in the next a 60-s run in a worker started for it, which one of the tries had made possible, waited 20.7 s for the call that hands files to copies, as such runs do from Vitest 4.1.9, where before it had a round to itself: 137.6 s for those rounds against 135.9 s, where a replay of the records had given 22 to 26 s less. The run took 457.1 s against 428.5 s, the rest being a 30-s run made with nothing else running for a mutant whose first leads came differently in the first rounds. Made again on formbricks `utils`, the tries of a file the round gave nothing else to do merged into a round with other work, it took 462.6 and 417.4 s against 401.1 to 444.4 s, in 18 rounds against 23: the round the tries of `validate-webhook-url.test.ts` joined waited for them, 45.3 s.
- **A new copy of a file for the tries left unclaimed,** made by the worker that was copied once its copy was through with the file, one copy at a time so that the file had no more processes at once than the plan gave it. A worker cannot go back to a test it has passed, and in rea one worker went through ten tries of 20 s each of a test the other workers of `ExecFileOutput.ts` had passed. It took 631 s against 641 s with `--led --confirm 4`, and 477 s against 482 s once such a file got as many workers as its tries: the test had been left to one worker because the file had three, and with more there is nobody left behind to start over for.
- **More of a file for its workers while the round is under way,** in place of waiting for the round to end to plan the next. A worker that had handed its file to a copy waited, once every run of the call to Vitest had its worker, for more of the file; the main process planned again from the records up to four times a second and added to the plan in flight what such a worker could take: tries of tests the round had not planned, runs of the whole file it had not, controls. Never a run in a worker started for it, a pair, a run with nothing else running or a test's one run in a copy, and nothing for a file about to be run one process at a time or measured otherwise: those rest on how the round began. Every try's claim to have had its file to itself was checked against when each process held the file, from what each wrote as it took the file on and when it, or the process it was copied from, saw it end: none of the 8,149 such tries of an immer run was wrong, and 7,541 of the 7,542 the plan had made next to others were seen as such. Replayed from the records with their durations, rounds with no barrier between them would have taken immer from 97 s to 77 s and es-toolkit `array` from 45 s to 38 s. On a quiet machine, three runs each in turn, immer took 104.3 to 110.1 s against 106.4 to 113.1 s and es-toolkit `array` 48.6 to 49.8 s against 47.2 to 48.4 s, every verdict as before. Before Vitest 4 no file is handed to a copy, so immer had no worker to give more to; from 4.1.9 the files that are handed over get a call of their own before the runs in workers started for them, and those runs are where es-toolkit's rounds spend their time. And what a round has learnt by its middle plans little: most of what the next round makes waits on what the end of a round resets, a mutant whose lead came to nothing going back to the others. The check of the tries cost a look at an immer run's records 41 ms against 25 ms and its records 5 in a hundred of their size, for nothing while the plan alone decides how many processes have a file.
- **One call to Vitest for a round from version 4.1.9 too,** every file then given a worker started for it, so that the files handed to copies do not wait for the runs in workers started for them, as before 4.1.9. On a quiet machine, three runs each in turn, es-toolkit `array` took 43.7 to 46.3 s against 44.9 to 48.2 s, the same at the median, with a tenth more processor time: the workers that hand their files over are no longer passed from file to file.
- **One run for two mutants that should fare alike,** counted from the reports of 15 scopes and not built. An `if` with no `else` whose condition is made `false` and the same `if` with its block emptied differ only in whether the condition is evaluated: of 1,805 such pairs one has two verdicts, and only the 208 pairs that survive would spare a run of every file each. A comparison or a logical operator swapped for another, against the same condition made `true` or `false`, does not hold at all: with both of those surviving the swap is detected often enough that nothing can be taken from them.
- **Ending a whole-file run that takes many times what the file takes unmutated,** as a timeout. Counted from the records: at ten times and at least a second it would have cut no run that passed, and saved 40% of the time in whole-file runs on query, 16% on es-toolkit and solid, 11% on h3, 8% on Effect. A limit in seconds gives another verdict on a machine that is behind, and what it saves in query is tests waiting for something that no longer comes, which no count of loop turns stands for.

- **Ending a run of a whole file as soon as its test can no longer finish,** where today it waits for Vitest's own time limit, five seconds as a rule. The sign was to be the one the workers already go by while tests try mutants: no processor time used and nothing keeping the event loop alive beyond what the worker itself holds, for half a second. Logged without acting on it, the sign was right every time it showed: 78 tests of query, 285 of solid, 24 of hono and 5 of jotai all ran into the limit afterwards, 1,700 s of waiting between them, most of solid's in the round that makes its runs one at a time. It is not sound all the same, and was not built. What Node lists as keeping the loop alive leaves out everything that was told not to: timers and sockets that are unref'd, among them Vitest's own timer for the limit, child processes, a timer made with `{ ref: false }`, which never passes through `unref`. It leaves out a worker thread altogether, a DNS query, and whatever a native addon does on a thread of its own. A test that waits for a thread to end, or for an unref'd child process, shows the sign and goes on 600 ms later; both were run. With one more timer allowed, as the check inside the workers allows for the limit's own, 50 tests of hono that sleep a second showed it and passed. That check has the same blind spots; what it finds is a lead, and a run that reaches Vitest's limit has to follow.
- **A copy made at the moment a mutant is first reached,** in the middle of a file's run, so that the tests before it are run once for all mutants (split-stream execution). Counted from the records and not built: the part of a passing whole-file run that comes before the first test to reach its mutant is 1% of all the time spent on such runs in hono, solid and Effect, 8% in jotai and immer, and 14% at most, in shiki. A copy made while a test is under way shares its open sockets and timers with the worker it came from, which is a good deal to take on for that.
- **Copies made after the file has loaded for mutants whose code runs while it loads but could change nothing there,** going by the probes of an unmutated run of the same file, with the copied process checking that its own load evaluated the same sites. Effect was what it was for: 42,000 of its whole-file runs are copies made before the file loads, 275 ms each against 40 ms, because the mutated modules run as every test file loads. It changed nothing: 42,148 such copies after, and no fewer on immer, jotai or vue. The mutants that survive there are of kinds the probes do not follow, a string or a function put in place of another, and a function defined as a module loads is a different function with the mutant on, whatever a probe could say. The verdicts were unchanged on five scopes; the fixture written for it (`limit`) is kept.
- **Making the second run of a failure with no other run of the file under way,** so that a file whose runs always fail each other would show a failure and then a pass. The second runs of a file then stand in one line: 67 s to 85 s on immer, 354 s to 462 s on solid, run in turn with the version before. And it did not reach the one place such failures were seen, in workers started for a run, whose second run comes a round later.
- **A copy with no mutant for every file before its copies are believed.** One run per file and kind of copy, and a file whose copy failed once by chance lost a round of runs: 7–17% on vue under load. It showed no wrong verdict anywhere. Such a copy is made only where one was lost.
- **Running a file in started workers after one failed unmutated copy.** On a busy machine one test failing by chance in that copy took a file of vue out of copying for the rest of the run, 10–30% of the whole time. It takes two such failures.
- **Seeing a failure twice in a round of its own.** With the rule that a lead only settles a failure of its own test, the second run first went to the next round: 110 s to 135–142 s on query, 54 s to 63–65 s on immer, 35 s to 40 s on es-toolkit, though only 6–27% of the detected mutants needed one. A round for a handful of runs costs what its slowest takes. Made on the spot by the worker that was copied, query is back at 109 s; immer stays at 61 s and es-toolkit at 40 s, which is what the rule costs where many mutants fail an earlier test of the file than the one that gave the lead.
- **Going on to the lead's test after another test failed,** so that one run would do: slower again, 147–155 s on query and 68–70 s on immer. The run that stops at the first failure and is made twice is cheaper than the one that goes on.
- **Making a failure that settled nothing once more on the spot.** That second run dates from when two failures in a row settled a mutant. Since they no longer do, it only ever told something when it passed, which it did 8 times in 3,184 over twenty scopes; what settled the others was the test that had failed trying the mutant with its file to itself, or the run with nothing else running, and both do that for one failure as for two. Without it, run in turn with the version before on a machine busy with other work: jotai 127 s and 129 s to 116 s and 121 s (whole-file runs 3,348 to 3,146), query 235 s to 213 s (4,382 to 3,911), the changed lines of excalidraw 326 s to 274 s (331 to 255), h3 735 s to 637 s (9,068 to 8,436), solid 667 s to 651 s (3,434 to 2,862, with 140 runs made with nothing else running where there were 85). Every verdict was as bench/truth/ has it.
- **No more trials for a test file whose workers found no lead in a round** (10 s of trials or more). svelte `sources` took 941 s with it and 941 s and 1,205 s without, and four lighter scopes did not change. svelte varies by a quarter between identical runs, so nothing is shown either way, and the rule is not kept.
- **Copies for mutants a test's own limits ended.** A mutant whose loop count or timer ended a test has not blocked a process, so its whole-file run could be a copy like any other. But in that run the loop goes on until it has kept the process busy for a second, and a worker makes its copies one after another: ufo's twelve such mutants took the run from 6.4 s to 9 s. They get workers started for them, which wait side by side.
- **Copies made at the first test that runs a mutant's code.** The worker went through the file's tests with no mutant on and copied itself, for each mutant, just before the test recorded as the first to reach it, so that the tests before it ran once and not once per mutant. Verdicts were the same on every target and CPU time fell by a tenth on excalidraw `math`, but no run got shorter (498 s against 496 s, immer 68 s against 62 s): a worker makes its copies one after another, and the tests most mutants are first reached by come early in a file.
- **Copies made the moment a mutant's code first runs while a file loads**, in place of copies made before loading. Each site was marked before its switch was read, and the worker, loading with no mutant on, copied itself on the first mark of a site that had runs waiting. Verdicts were the same on every target and no run got shorter: in immer, where half the mutants run while some test file loads, 538 of 2,863 runs were made this way and the rest were copies made after loading already. Libraries load little before the module under test. Applications load a framework first, and there copies made as the mutant's module begins its own code are kept, see [Making such a process cheap](design.md#making-such-a-process-cheap).
- **Waiting, while a file loads, for what is under way to end before copying there**, as copies made after loading do. A second agent built two runs that this got wrong on Vite's module runner: a dependency's chain of promises ran to its end in the wait and set a flag the module's code then read, and a function of the module called through an import cycle before the module began had made a value with the mutant off. The copies are made on the spot instead, and not at all where a site of the module has run.
- **Dropping the round in which every test tries what is left**, now that a whole-file run is a copy. Verdicts were the same; the other targets ran between as long and a seventh shorter, excalidraw `math` went from 498 s to 560 s. A whole-file run remains a costly way to find the test that fails where the tests are heavy.
- **Keeping workers alive between files** (`isolate: false` after the first round) to save re-importing the application. With Vitest 3.0, every file after the first in a reused worker collected no tests at all. That worker had run the file before; a worker that only copies itself for each file has run none, see [item 14](design.md#making-such-a-process-cheap).
- **Comparing results of side-effect-free functions.** The unmutated run called each such function again with each mutant on and skipped the test if every call returned the same. It ruled out 22% of the pairs it checked, while the first round grew from 27 s to 174 s.
- **Sharing one run among same-site mutants with identical value traces.** At most 5% of survive runs could have been saved.
- **Capping the tests tried per mutant.** Trying even the ten cheapest covering tests first finds the killer for only 42–63% of killed mutants, so a cap would turn kills into survivors.
- **Evening out when each test gets its turn within a file.** A kill matrix of the 174 `math` mutants detected after the coverage round showed that tests ran them 5,047 times without killing, where trying the tests in random order would expect 2,171: the tests at the end of long files run last, and a mutant only they can kill has been run by every other test by then. Cutting each file into stretches taken in shuffled order brought that down to 3,369 in one run and 4,938 in the next, with no gain in time, and unbalanced the `pr` scope. Starting each file's workers at staggered places instead was slower on both scopes. Shuffling whole worker runs in long rounds, which was kept, brings it to about 3,800.
- **Trying likely killers first.** On the same matrix, ordering tests by how many other mutants they kill does worse than random for the mutants that are hard to kill (302 s of test time against 219 s), and no test that killed a neighbouring mutant earlier turned out to be the killer of one that wasted runs.
- **Trying mutants only after a coverage round that tries none**, so that no test's unmutated run comes after a mutant. Verdicts were the same on all four targets, and excalidraw `math` took 596 s instead of 497 s: the mutants that fast tests detect in passing then need workers of their own. Going through the files once more before the whole-file runs costs about 16 s there.
- **Skipping the round in which every test tries what is left** and sending those mutants straight to whole-file runs. The same verdicts; immer and jotai gained 2 s to 4 s, excalidraw `math` went from 485 s to 615 s, because a whole-file run is a costly way to find a test that fails.
- **Checking a pass or an uncertain failure in a fresh worker with the mutant on only during the test in question.** The whole-file run with the mutant on from the start settles the same cases and the ones this missed, so the separate check, and the status for what it left open, were removed with no verdict changing.
- **The `vmForks` pool.** Workers that keep their process and get a fresh VM context per file ran the plain suite in 20.5 s instead of 25.1 s, but 15 of the benchmark's tests fail under it, and a pool that changes what tests do changes verdicts.
- **Telling workers where served modules are on disk**, so that they do not ask the main process for each one (Vitest before 4). Startup went from 1.4 s to 1.1 s per worker and the runs got no shorter.
- **Two or three first tries per mutant.** Giving each mutant to its top tests by reached code at once left fewer killable mutants for the main round (`math` 163–168 s against about 173 s) and added work where the first try already kills most (`pr` 64.5 s against 63.1 s). It also moved three mutants from Killed to Timeout, since two tests now reach a mutant at once.
- **A higher threshold for running mutants during the coverage round** (`--cheap-ms`): 40 and 60 ms were within noise of 20 ms, and from 80 ms on files ran out of their budget in the first round and the run took several times longer.
- **Other orders of worker runs**: files with a single run first, a few workers reserved for short runs. Each was slower.
- **More workers for small rounds.** Letting a round with 12 s of work use all 16 workers instead of 6 made it longer (5.5 s to 6.6 s): each worker imports the application first.
- **A V8 code cache for the modules Vitest evaluates itself**, compiled with `cachedData`: startup of an excalidraw worker went from 1.4 s to 1.3 s and the coverage round stayed where it was.
- **Leaving V8 some of its threads in workers that are copied,** not built. A copy has none of them, so each kind of work left to them would need showing that no copy waits for it. What there is to gain was measured first, on the plain suite of excalidraw, the heaviest here, two runs each on twelve cores: 25.5 and 25.3 s as V8 starts, 27.2 and 27.3 s with `--single-threaded`, 25.8 and 25.7 s with only the garbage collector on one thread, 26.8 and 26.9 s with only compiling in the background off. Seven in a hundred, most of it the compiler's, and the lines changed in excalidraw take 207.0 s with copies and 214.8 s without, one after the other on that machine.
- **More runs under way than there are cores where the runs wait,** counted and not built. Going by the processor time and the time on the clock of each test file with no mutant on, the runs of whole files spend much of their time waiting for a timer or a server and not computing: 27 in a hundred in h3 on a 4-core Linux runner, where four of its files wait for nine tenths of theirs, 12 in Effect, where one file takes 17 s and 0.4 s of processor time, 54 in jotai, 8 in excalidraw. The figures from macOS runners are higher throughout, by about what shiki shows there for files that wait for nothing, and were not used. Each such run holds one of as many places as there are cores. Letting a round begin runs until the shares of a core they are expected to use add up to the cores would at best, with no run slowing another, have taken 16 in a hundred off the whole run of h3, 8 off Effect's and 4 off excalidraw's. That is with the files that wait given more processes each, which is more of what "One test file runs in many processes at once" under [Limits](limits.md) is about, and the files that wait are those with a server on a port. With every file's runs kept one after another it is 10 in a hundred in h3, 6 in Effect and nothing in excalidraw: what bounds a round is then the one file with the most runs to make. Against either stand a worker more to keep in memory for every place added, and timers that come due late for every test that checks one.
- **The cheap files of a mutant's first four before the dear ones,** counted and not built. A mutant with no lead gets four files in one round, and since a failure in one keeps the others from being begun, a file that takes 16 s begun before one that fails in under a second is 16 s for nothing. In Effect that is 8 in a hundred of the time spent on runs of whole files on Linux and 7 on macOS, nearly all of it one test file of its cluster package; in h3 it is 1, and in shiki, jotai and excalidraw nothing. A round's runs are given out by file and not by mutant, the longest first so that the round does not end on one, and holding a file's runs back for the mutants that have a cheaper file under way would be a rule for one project.
- **Runs made with nothing else running, several at once, each of a different test file,** a quarter of the cores at most, considered and not built. The plain suite runs more files than that at once, so a failure there is one it could show; but two files that bind the same port or write the same path may never meet in the suite and would meet here, a kill no rule today can give, and a stop by the watchdog would still have to be made again alone, CPU time stretching on a busy core for a mutant that is only slow. In rea's `ReverseEngineeringService.ts` such runs were 326 s of 677 s over three files, most of them stops by the watchdog; in `ExecFileOutput.ts` one file, nothing to gain. Made three at a time once, in a copy of the tool that was not kept, the round took 196 s and the run 508 s against 643 s, every verdict as before but for a Killed that became a Timeout: what it would take is a run going wrong on two files that share a port, which one run does not show either way.
- **Taking a run the watchdog stopped for a timeout where a witness of the file hung by this tool's own limits,** as a run that blocked a worker while a test tried the mutant already is. Counted and not built. In rea's `ReverseEngineeringService.ts` it would have spared 15 of 22 runs made one at a time, 12 of them on witnesses ended by the timer that waits on a test with nothing using the processor. But a timer that runs out and a run the clock ends are both what a busy machine can make of a mutant that only slows a child process down, which the suite passes; the rule for a blocked worker holds because both of its stops are counted in processor time. Only the witness that sat with nothing open and the stop by the clock would match, three of the 15. A stop on time on the processor or on memory, which a busy machine does not bring about, does name its test now, as a loop this runner ends in the test does ([Deciding](design.md#deciding), item 8).
- **Believing a failure without the run made alone where the test read no clock and waited for nothing,** counted and not built. What a busy machine can do to a test would have to be listed in full, every clock, timer, descriptor and collection of garbage, and one way left out is a mutant reported as killed that the suite passes with. The rounds of runs made alone are what it could save, and on the Linux runners they were 16 in a hundred of solid's time, 10 of immer's and zustand's, 9 of jotai's and 8 or less elsewhere, and the tests of solid, zustand and jotai run in jsdom, which sets timers of its own.
- **No tries for a mutant whose runs of a file only ran out of the time a test is given,** before its run made with nothing else running. Such a try mostly runs out of the time too, which settles nothing alone, and in formbricks the tries of mutants that leave a DNS callback uncalled held one worker for tens of seconds. Left out, formbricks `utils` took 518.6 s against 476.3 s: the tries had given leads, and without them the round made one run at a time had 81 runs with a mutant and 49 with none, against 77 and 41, and took 156 s against 120 s. Every verdict was as before but for two that both fail a test and loop, Killed and Timeout.
- **Not running again, one process at a time, the runs of a file that no other run of it overlapped,** once the file is found to be one whose runs fail each other. Counted and not built: in a run of actual `server`, 17 of the 252 runs of `executors.test.ts` overlapped no other run of that file, where its 486 runs made one process at a time in another run took 1,503 s.
- **Ending a test in a run of a whole file once nothing is left that could settle what it waits for,** as tries do after half a second with no CPU in use and nothing held beyond the worker's own. On jotai's `vanilla`, 38 mutants each stop one async test at Vitest's 5 s, twice, about 380 s of a 3-core runner's time where the 1-second limit of before took a tenth of that and was not exact. A second agent built four tests that look that idle and pass well within the limit: one waiting for a timer that was unref'd, which Node does not list as keeping anything alive, one for a child process, one for a chain of `setImmediate` and one for `Atomics.waitAsync` with a time of its own. The run of a whole file is the one that settles a verdict, so the test runs to Vitest's limit there.
- **Ending a loop past its count once the clock alone has passed the test's limit.** Vitest fails a test that returns after its limit, so such a loop fails the suite however it ends; but on a busy machine the clock passes the limit while the loop has done far less than on a quiet one, where it may end in time and pass. The loop has to have kept the process busy for the limit as well. On a 3-core runner that took es-toolkit's one endless loop in `pr` 10 s, where the second of before took 1.1 s and was not exact.
- **A loop past its count looking every 10 ms, not every million iterations, whether it may end,** built and taken out. On a macOS runner valibot's runs that loop had taken 12 s to 21 s for a limit of 5 s, which looked like the look coming late where each iteration made objects to keep. In one job with the version before, three runs each, it took ufo 49.7 to 53.8 s against 45.8 to 55.4 s, valibot 305.5 to 329.1 s against 308.7 to 311.0 s and es-toolkit `pr` 54.8 to 61.0 s against 47.8 to 56.0 s on macOS, and 1.00 to 1.05 times the time before on Linux; on this machine valibot's looping runs took 6.35 s against 6.45 s. Nothing it was for showed.
- **Handing files to copies of one worker on Vitest 3,** built and taken out. Vitest 3 takes the pool's options once, as it makes the pool, so a round that hands its files over made the pool again with `isolate` off, for projects whose environment is Vitest's `node` alone, which sets up and takes down one property of `console`. On immer, three workers on this machine, the workers started went from 350 to 179 and the run from 168.8 s to 174.2 s, every verdict as before: a worker of immer is cheap to start, and a third of its time is runs that wait out Vitest's limit for a loop.
- **Making copies write fewer of the pages they share with the worker,** measured and not built. A copy pays for each page it first writes to, which on a macOS runner is 6.6 to 11.4 µs of system time a page against 1.3 µs on this Mac. Counted in the copies themselves (`faults`, `userMs`, `systemMs` on a copy's record of its run), the runs of immich `services` and `utils` in copies took 9,166 to 11,301 faults at the median and spent a fifth of their time in the system: 897 s of 4,360, 785 of 3,998, 989 of 4,868. The copies' runs are under half the run's worker time, so no fault at all would save under a tenth. Turning V8's memory reducer off in the copies took immich `album` from 26,194 faults at the median to 24,540, 31.7 s to 30.6 s; collecting the worker's garbage just before copying it hung the copy that followed, a thread of the collector being at work as it was made. Nor does it tell the one run of immich `services` in thirteen that took 1,731 s from the others, 2,869 to 4,099 s: `bench/forkcost.mjs` found a copy's collection slower than its parent's on each of the six runners it ran on, all of them slow ones, by as much as it varied between copies on one runner.

Run with 4, 32 and 48 workers on 12 cores, zustand, hono `router`, ufo, jotai, query and pinia give the verdicts of the 16-worker run for every mutant but that difference of name: one of jotai's 1,160 and at most three of query's 2,804 change between Killed and Timeout, and none moves between detected and survived.
